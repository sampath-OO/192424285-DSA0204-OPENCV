Academic & Technical Overview

B.Tech Computer Vision with OpenCV

1. Image Preprocessing & Camera Calibration

Images are decoded with cv2.imread and normalized. A pinhole intrinsic matrix \(K\) with radial distortion coefficients (\(k_1, k_2\)) is applied via cv2.undistort. If calibration parameters are absent, safe fallback maintains pipeline stability.

2. Road Markings & Lane Detection

Implemented using classical OpenCV techniques: Grayscale conversion \(\rightarrow\) Gaussian Blur \((5\times 5)\) \(\rightarrow\) Canny Edge Detection \((50, 150)\) \(\rightarrow\) Road Trapezoid ROI Mask \(\rightarrow\) Probabilistic Hough Line Transform (cv2.HoughLinesP).

3. Object & Pedestrian Detection

A lightweight single-stage YOLOv8 neural network detects vehicles (cars, buses, trucks, motorcycles, bicycles) and pedestrians with bounding boxes and confidence scores. Pedestrians positioned inside the roadway corridor trigger high-priority safety warnings.

4. Road Sign Recognition

Identifies regulatory and warning signs (Stop signs, Traffic lights, Speed limit indicators) using neural classes combined with OpenCV contour circularity and HSV color segmentation.

5. Occlusion Handling (Current vs Future)

Implemented: Single-frame 2D bounding box intersection over union (IoU > 0.30) to detect spatial occlusion.
Future Extension: Temporal Kalman Filter multi-object tracking and DeepSORT re-identification across sequential video frames.

6. Video Processing & Tracking (Future Scope)

This prototype intentionally focuses on single-image evaluation for deterministic, reliable viva demonstrations. Multi-object tracking (MOT) and real-time RTSP video streaming represent direct future extensions.
